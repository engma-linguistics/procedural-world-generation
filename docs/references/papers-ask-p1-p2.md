# Papers to ask for (P2 population by way of life; P1 settlement sizes)

All references below were found online on 2026-10-30 (search results and publisher/PMC pages). DOIs come from those pages. "OA" = open access.

## P2: how many people the land feeds

Ranked, most useful first.

1. **Zhu, D., et al. (2021).** Global hunter-gatherer population densities constrained by influence of seasonality on diet composition. *Nature Ecology & Evolution* 5(11): 1536-1545. DOI 10.1038/s41559-021-01548-3. OA: yes (author manuscript https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7611941). Full author list: not verified.
   - Settles: annual NPP explains under 30% of forager density, with about 1,000-fold unexplained spread per unit NPP, so it shows how far a 0-5 foraging score can be pushed and why short seasons cut density.
2. **Tallavaara, M., Eronen, J. T., & Luoto, M. (2018).** Productivity, biodiversity, and pathogens influence the global hunter-gatherer population density. *PNAS* 115(6). DOI 10.1073/pnas.1715638115 (DOI taken from the authors' blog post, not the PNAS page). OA: yes (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5819417).
   - Settles: how forager density scales with NPP, and where biodiversity (poor land) and pathogens (rich tropical land) flatten the curve, which is the concavity question for the "humans live nearly anywhere" assumption.
3. **Binford, L. R. (2001).** *Constructing Frames of Reference: An Analytical Method for Archaeological Theory Building Using Ethnographic and Environmental Data Sets.* University of California Press, Berkeley. ISBN 0520223934, 563 pp. (Subtitle varies by source: "Hunter-Gatherer and Environmental Data Sets" in some catalogues.) OA: no.
   - Settles: the ground-truth forager, and some herder, density table (339 groups) that PW-833 and PW-1020 cite but do not have on file; the owner's call on orders-of-magnitude spread depends on it.
4. **Kavanagh, P. H., Vilela, B., Haynie, H. J., Tuff, T., Lima-Ribeiro, M. S., Gray, R. D., Botero, C. A., & Gavin, M. C. (2018).** Hindcasting global population densities reveals forces enabling the origin of agriculture. *Nature Human Behaviour* 2(7): 478-484. DOI not verified (the IDEAS record suggests 10.1038/s41562-018-0358-8). OA: not verified.
   - Settles: a published climate-to-density model fitted on recent foragers and run back 21 kyr, a direct template for a density-from-environment function.
5. **Klein Goldewijk, K., Beusen, A., Doelman, J., & Stehfest, E. (2017).** Anthropogenic land use estimates for the Holocene - HYDE 3.2. *Earth System Science Data* 9: 927-953. https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/9/927/2017/ . OA: yes (CC BY 3.0). Data: DOI 10.17026/dans-25g-gez3.
   - Settles: global and gridded population and farmland per era (4.4 M at 10,000 BCE to 7.26 B at 2015), so a farmer-density-vs-productivity check against reconstructions.
   - Also useful: Klein Goldewijk et al. (2011), HYDE 3.1, *Global Ecology and Biogeography* 20(1): 73-86, DOI 10.1111/j.1466-8238.2010.00587.x; PDF at https://geo.public.data.uu.nl/vault-hyde-data/HYDE%203.1%5B1655214378%5D/original/Klein_Goldewijk_et_al_2011-GEB.pdf .
6. **Zimmermann, A., Hilpert, J., & Wendt, K. P. (2009).** Estimations of population density for selected periods between the Neolithic and AD 1800. *Human Biology* 81(2-3): 357-380. DOI 10.3378/027.081.0313 (from the BioOne URL). OA: no.
   - Settles: regional farmer densities (Bandkeramik, Iron Age, Roman, Merovingian, AD 1800) from site data. The authors report non-state figures are always lower than conventional estimates, so it caps what early farmers should reach. The author list in the owner's memory ("Richter, Hinz") was wrong.
7. **Tallavaara, M., Luoto, M., Korhonen, N., Jarvinen, H., & Seppa, H. (2015).** Human population dynamics in Europe over the Last Glacial Maximum. *PNAS* 112(27): 8232-8237. DOI 10.1073/pnas.1503784112. OA: yes (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4500234).
   - Settles: a worked model of forager density from a productivity proxy, with absolute numbers (about 410,000 people in Europe at 13 ka), a sanity check for the absolute scale.
8. **Hamilton, M. J., Milne, B. T., Walker, R. S., & Brown, J. H. (2007).** Nonlinear scaling of space use in human hunter-gatherers. *PNAS* 104(11): 4765-4769. DOI 10.1073/pnas.0611197104. OA: yes (author PDF https://www.unm.edu/~robwal/pnas.pdf).
   - Settles: area per person scales with group size at about the 0.70 power, a nonlinear (concave) relation, relevant to whether density should be strictly linear in the layer. Exponent figures came from secondary summaries; check the PDF.
9. **Jean-Richard, V., Crump, L., et al. (2015).** Estimating population and livestock density of mobile pastoralists and sedentary settlements in the south-eastern Lake Chad area. *Geospatial Health* 10(1): 6-12. DOI not verified; full author list not verified. OA: not verified.
   - Settles: a measured herder density (5.9-17.5 people/km2 mobile, dry season; 64 people/km2 sedentary villages) and 86.6 TLU/km2, which is up to 5x published Sahel carrying capacities (14-23 TLU/km2). That is a rare real herder number to test the grazing-NPP rule against.
10. **Kelly, R. L. (2013).** *The Lifeways of Hunter-Gatherers: The Foraging Spectrum.* Cambridge University Press (2nd ed.; earlier editions 1995, 2004). Chapter 7 "Group Size and Demography", pp. 166-213. ISBN not verified. OA: no.
   - Settles: reviewed forager density and demography; low priority if Binford is obtained.

Not on the list: Boserup (1965) *The Conditions of Agricultural Growth* exists but is theory without density-by-productivity numbers (first-edition publisher not verified). Behnke (2000) and Ellis & Swift (1988) on non-equilibrium rangelands (rainfall variability, not NPP, caps herds) surfaced but their references are not verified. No clean herder-density-vs-NPP paper was found; item 9 is the closest.

## P1: settlement sizes through history

**Recommended single source: Reba, M., Reitsma, F., & Seto, K. C. (2016).** Spatializing 6,000 years of global urbanization from 3700 BC to AD 2000. *Scientific Data* 3: 160034. DOI 10.1038/sdata.2016.34. OA: yes (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4896125/). Data: NASA SEDAC, DOI 10.7927/H4ZG6QBX.
- Why: one open paper plus a downloadable table (city, coordinates, year, population; 1,741 cities, 10,353 city/date/population values) built from Chandler 1987 and Modelski 2003, so it gives the largest city in every era.
- Important limit (read from the open copy, Background & Summary): it holds only the biggest cities. Chandler lists cities over 20,000 for AD 800-1850 (40,000 for Asia), and over 40,000 everywhere after 1850. Modelski's minimums are 10,000 for 3500-1000 BC, 100,000 for 1000 BC-AD 1000 and 1,000,000 from AD 1000. So it cannot fix village or town bands; it can fix "city" and "largest city" per era.
- Size bands: the open text gives no per-era table (per-era data are in its Supplementary Tables 2-8, which I could not read). The only examples are Beijing as the sole million-city in AD 1800 and Istanbul at 400,000-500,000 in AD 500 (Usage Notes, item 18). I will not invent bands. No page refs are possible beyond those section names.
- Honest answer to the owner: no single paper settles village, town and city bands. The dataset settles the top of the scale only.

**Runner-up (for the lower bands): Fletcher, R. (1995).** *The Limits of Settlement Growth: A Theoretical Outline.* Cambridge University Press (New Studies in Archaeology), ISBN 9780521038102, about 304 pp. OA: no.
- Why: reviews settlement growth worldwide over 15,000 years and the size ceilings at each stage (mobile to sedentary to urban to industrial), which is the village-to-town range that Reba/Chandler omit. I could not read its tables, so the actual sizes are not verified.

**Other candidates, verified to exist:**
- Cesaretti, R., Lobo, J., Bettencourt, L. M. A., Ortman, S. G., & Smith, M. E. (2016). Population-area relationship for medieval European cities. *PLoS ONE* 11(10): e0162678. OA: yes (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5051806). Population and area for 173 European cities c. 1300; dataset on tDAR. Good for medieval town and city size distribution.
- Ortman, S. G., Cabaniss, A. H. F., Sturm, J. O., & Bettencourt, L. M. A. (2014). The pre-history of urban scaling. *PLoS ONE* 9(2): e87902. DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0087902. OA: yes (PLoS). Basin of Mexico settlements; shows ancient settlements scale like modern ones. Does not give bands. 
- Chandler, T. (1987). *Four Thousand Years of Urban Growth: An Historical Census.* Edwin Mellen Press. OA: no. Reba's source; criticised for uncritical sources.
- Modelski, G. (2003). *World Cities: -3000 to 2000.* FAROS, Washington DC, 245 pp. OA: not verified (a review is at https://irows.ucr.edu/cd/reviews/modrev.htm).
- Bairoch, P. (1988). *Cities and Economic Development: From the Dawn of History to the Present.* University of Chicago Press, tr. C. Braider, xxii+574 pp. OA: no (one catalogue lists a Mansell, London edition). Not read.

Ask-list suggestion for P1: Reba 2016 (free, grab the SEDAC data) plus Fletcher 1995 (book).
