Viewer UX side-by-sides

Lane 53, 2026-10-30. Three looks for you to choose between. Each option is built and working behind a switch you add to the viewer's address, and none of them is the default yet. Pictures are of planet 3097e3 (a peoples run on world 2a7385), world 2a7385 and zoom 80a169.

A. Peoples' colours (PW-1032)

The peoples layer gives each people a colour. Today several neighbours share a colour, and some colours vanish into the ocean blue or the grey of empty land. Try it in the viewer: add &palette=distinct or &palette=family to a peoples run's address.

1. Current

The palette as it is: 35 colours for 66 peoples, picked by hand.

2. Neighbours always distinct

Each people gets the colour furthest from its neighbours', checked for colour-blind sight too. Names are ignored, so red dragons can come out green.

3. Family hues + distinct neighbours

Dragons warm, giants blue, fey violet; colour-named peoples keep their colour (red dragons red, gold dragons gold); then neighbours are pushed apart.

"Look alike" means a colour difference (CIELAB ΔE) under 10 for peoples that share a border or overlap at any time in the run; colour-blind sight is simulated for red- and green-blindness (Machado et al. 2009). Current's lost colours include red dragons, elves and tritons.

Recommendation: option 3, family hues. It fixes every clash the distinct palette fixes and keeps the meaning in the names: red dragons are red, the giants read as one family. One wrinkle: black dragons cannot be black on a dark map, so the clash pass gives them a dark rose. If you prefer, they can be a fixed charcoal with a light outline instead.

B. Colour ramps (PW-1027)

For "how much" maps the eye reads brightness as the amount. Several ramps get lighter and then darker again, so the least and the most look alike (the darkest wave-power red is about as dark as the calmest sea). The proposal keeps each ramp's hues and makes its brightness run one way only. The zoom also switches to the globe's temperature, rain and fertility ramps, so the same place looks the same in both. Try it: add &ramps=new to the globe's or the zoom's address.

Recommendation: take the shared zoom ramps now, and the one-way rule ramp by ramp. Wave power, river floods, tide range, upwelling, building ground and soil depth read better straight away. Cloud and humidity barely change. Fire return comes out muddy (its "never burns" stop sits at one end of a log scale, and the rule darkens its reds): that one is better hand-tuned than rebuilt by the rule.

C. Docked legend (PW-1029)

Today the legend floats at the bottom of the map and can cover the map, especially with peoples or event switches showing. The option moves it into the right-hand column, just above the summary. Try it: add &legend=dock to the globe's address.

Recommendation: dock it. The map gets its bottom edge back and the legend sits next to the summary that explains the same layer. The cost is a shorter summary on small screens; the summary already folds away with its title button.
