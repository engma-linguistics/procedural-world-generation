Climate v1, in two parts

Why Earth-likes weren't reliably Earth-like

Part 1 is the short answer, ranked by how much each cause mattered. Part 2 is the week as a log, for when you want to check a detail. Every line comes from the board, the decisions page or the lane notes.

Part 1: What it was

Four findings from the last day, then the earlier ones.

1. Worlds froze over and could not thaw PW-1142, PW-1156, PW-1162

What was wrong. About a quarter of the test worlds went "frozen over" (40% or more of the land under ice): 4 of 18 on the old code. Real ice ages end because the orbit periodically gives the northern summers extra sun, and the big sheets melt. The long run cannot step through those orbits, so it used the average orbit. A big sheet is high and bright and makes its own cold air, so under the average it never got the warm summer that starts a melt. The sheets outlasted the climate that made them. Lane 71 also found that in three of the four frozen worlds the freeze began with CO2 falling about 8-fold in roughly 12 Myr, deep enough to reach ice-age cold whatever the sheet did.

What fixed it. Three changes together (PW-1142, built as PW-1162): the cooling-with-height rate is gentler where ice is melting; a sheet carried from a cold era keeps only what would survive the warm phase of the orbit; melting ice is dark bare ice and takes in more sun. On 18 test worlds: 0 frozen over in the window that counts, 12 of 18 pass (the old code passed 11). One cost: Earth's own Greenland shrinks to 68 cells (from 165). You accepted that. Ice flow is the real cure and is v2.

2. Freezes under the dim young Sun are Earth-like history your ruling, 10-30

What was wrong. Not the physics but the bar. The Sun was dimmer when the planet was young, and the early freezes it allows are the kind real Earth history has. Failing a world for them was failing it for being like Earth.

What fixed it. You ruled that frozen over is a hard fail only while the Sun is near its final brightness (within about 2 C of the finish, or the last 300 Myr, whichever window is narrower). Earlier frozen spells are allowed. Also, only "frozen over" fails a world now; "icehouse" and "glacial maximum" are reported, not failed (PW-1091).

3. A CO2 crash at the start of every run's last stretch lane 67

What was wrong. A bookkeeping bug. The rock-weathering supply was kept per time step, but the first step of the finish window read a 2 Myr step's erosion times 4. Every run's CO2 crashed to 3 to 50 ppm at 990.5 Myr, so every world's "present" came out colder than it should. That also hid the worlds that were really too warm.

What fixed it. The supply is now kept by the step it was eroded in. With the crash gone, some worlds finish 3.5 to 5.3 C too warm (PW-1161), which led to finding 4.

4. The remaining spread is natural, not a bug PW-1158, PW-1163, PW-1161, PW-1164, lane 80

What it is. Finish temperatures centre near 0 C with a spread of about 3.7 C. Lane 80 traced it to slow, Earth-like wandering of volcanic CO2 and of erosion, made bigger by ice and methane feedback. Earth's own record swings more. No model change. Two real biases remain, and they cancel: the volcanic supply sinks late in some runs (about 1.6 C too cold, PW-1158), and the weathering reference is learnt on bare ground before plants exist, which erodes up to 1.6x faster (about 1.6 C too warm, PW-1163). Fixing only one would swap warm failures for cold ones, so they are fixed together in v2.

One decision is yours: keep the +3 C warm edge of the finish band, or move it to about +4.7 C (PW-1164, desk D19). At +3 C roughly 1 world in 5 lands too warm; at about +4.7 C, about 1 in 10.

Earlier causes, roughly by weight

5. Ice sheets depended on the step size; Antarctica's summer overshot PW-1050, PW-1095

The ice-climate loop advanced one step per update, so the number of iceboxes depended on how often climate ran. It now settles inside each update, a double-counted summer was removed, and a winter-only sheet inversion was added (passes 78%). That left Antarctica's interior summer too warm. Scaling the summer loss by the melt share fixed it: warmest month -12.3 to -14.0 C (observed -16.1); 14 of 18 pass vs 13.

6. Dry coasts and the sea's water PW-998, PW-015, PW-980

A coast package with poleward eddy drift (Earth improved; passes 72%; more realistic polar snow made 8 of 18 worlds "iceboxes", mostly Earth-warm ones). Sea evaporation became a bulk exchange (Earth sea share 27 to 28% vs 30 observed). The continents' sea water is now conserved (null at L6, 29/36 vs 28/36).

7. Thin-crust continents and plates that never split PW-970, PW-565, PW-952

The weld fix: a welded plate now needs an inlet on its own grid and at least 20 km of surrounding crust, because a sampling ratchet was growing continents with thin crust (pass 31/36 to 28/36, every loss warm). Stress rifts stopped dying out, so the largest plate went from 41% to 26%.

8. Monsoons and mid-latitude wetness: understood, parked PW-1093, PW-1099, PW-1100

One mechanism makes both rain errors: the summer sea-to-land inflow carries the whole vapour column inland. A patch is written up, but the coasts lack vapour supply, not lift, so it waits for a partner in v2. Retuning (0.45, drift, rain rates) was declined.
