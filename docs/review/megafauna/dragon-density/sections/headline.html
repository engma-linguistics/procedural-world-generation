The headline: dragons by scale and competition

How many dragons can a world, a region and a biome hold, and how does that change as other mouths compete for the same food? Each row below adds one kind of competition, and each is a setting in the code (the lineages settings in the viewer), with the default marked. The numbers are for the present, as medians over the six worlds at roll 7000. Dragons of every kind and age are counted, wyrmlings included. A Sword Coast-sized region ({{ sig(N['headline']['region_km2']/1e6,2) }} million km²) is taken at the whole land's average density.

Three things set the numbers.

  • Rival predators matter most to what the food can feed. The guild of smaller carnivores eats about {{ sig(N['headline']['guild_ratio'],2) }} times what the dragons eat, so with neither that guild nor the giants the food would feed about {{ sig(N['headline']['rows']['alone']['capacity']['world']/N['headline']['rows']['rivals']['capacity']['world'],2) }} times as many dragons.
  • Habitat and viability matter most to what the worlds hold. The model's worlds hold about {{ pct(N['headline']['rows']['hunt']['realised']['world']/N['headline']['rows']['hunt']['capacity']['world'],2) }} of what the food would feed even with the pressure off. Each dragon kind lives only in its own kinds of country, and a pocket too small to last fades out (Section 5.3).
  • Among the peoples' channels:
    • the hunters' take and the overkill each roughly halve the food, and they hit hunters' country hardest (Table 4);
    • the kill matters only in the late eras, and then mostly in farmed land. At the default it takes the world from {{ sig(N['headline']['rows']['overkill']['realised']['world'],2) }} dragons to {{ sig(N['headline']['rows']['persecution']['realised']['world'],2) }}. A gentler or harsher setting moves that to {{ sig(N['headline']['rows']['press_0.25']['realised']['world'],2) }} or {{ sig(N['headline']['rows']['press_4']['realised']['world'],2) }}.

The rest of the paper explains each row. Section 2 sets out the food chain and the age-by-age kill. Section 5 splits the loss by cause, era and way of life. Section 9 lists what the literature says should change.
