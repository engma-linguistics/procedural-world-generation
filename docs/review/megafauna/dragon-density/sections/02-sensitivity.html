Three things follow. First, the intake law alone moves density by a factor of four ({{ X(S('Mammal')['factor']) }} to {{ X(S('Varanid lizards')['factor']) }}), more than the gap between the model and any literature line in Section 4.5. Flight would push intake toward the bird line (fewer dragons); no paper on file gives a daily cost of flight. Second, the unsourced generous choices (85% assimilation, whole carcasses) and the reptile-fit intake pull in opposite directions; a reading that follows the papers at every step (the first joint case) lands at about twice the model's density. Third, the above-ground share is the largest single unknown, and it pushes one way only: toward fewer dragons on grassland. The model's 1 per {{ sig(N['chain']['one_per_km2_at_1t'],3) }} km² is therefore a central value inside a band of roughly a factor of ten either way, not a measurement.
