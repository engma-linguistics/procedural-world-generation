3. How sure is the headline?

The headline, 1 dragon per {{ sig(N['chain']['one_per_km2_at_1t'],3) }} km² for each t/km² of prey where nobody else eats, is a product of a few constants. Each row below changes one of them to a value from a paper consulted (or to an illustrative value, where marked) and gives the factor on dragon density. The last row is different in kind: it changes how much prey a given amount of plant growth yields, so it moves density at a given place but not the headline per tonne of prey. Nothing is re-run: density is proportional to each of these factors, so the arithmetic is exact.

Three things follow. First, the intake law alone moves density by a factor of four ({{ X(S('Mammal')['factor']) }} to {{ X(S('Varanid lizards')['factor']) }}), more than the {{ sig(1/(89.1*1650**-1.0/10)/N['chain']['one_per_km2_at_1t'],2) }} to {{ sig(N['literature']['carbone_one_per_km2_at_1t']/N['chain']['one_per_km2_at_1t'],2) }} times between the model and Carbone & Gittleman's prey-based line (Section 5.4). Flight would push intake toward the bird line (fewer dragons); no paper consulted gives a daily cost of flight. Second, the unsourced generous choices (85% assimilation, whole carcasses) and the reptile-fit intake pull in opposite directions; a reading that follows the papers at every step (the first joint case, with McNab's own dinosaur intake) lands at {{ X(N['sensitivity']['combined'][1]['factor']) }}, close to the model. The closeness is partly a coincidence: an intake law stretched far past its data happens to offset two unsourced generous choices. Third, the headline per tonne of prey is uncertain mostly through the intake law, but the prey itself carries a second, one-sided unknown: the above-ground share, which can only lower density at a given place, and most on grassland. Counting it, the lowest joint case falls from {{ X(N['sensitivity']['combined'][2]['factor']) }} to {{ X(N['sensitivity']['combined'][4]['factor']) }}. The model's 1 per {{ sig(N['chain']['one_per_km2_at_1t'],3) }} km² per t/km² is therefore a central value inside a band of roughly a factor of ten either way (the top of it an upper bound), with a further factor of up to four downward on grassland from the prey formula; it is not a measurement.
