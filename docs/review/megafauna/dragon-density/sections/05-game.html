6. The game present: dragons by land and settlement

This section is an extrapolation, not a model result. A typical D&D game sits at about late-medieval technology, the 1400s or so, and the generator's history of its peoples is designed to stop at about that stage by default. It does not reach that far yet: the runs here end with peoples of an earlier stage. The table below takes the model's present-day densities by land type and settlement, then reasons about what the 1400s would change. Every step of that reasoning is stated, and the best-guess column contains a judgement of this review, not the model's.

Situations. Each land cell of every run's present is put in one of four situations:

  • Wild: nobody's country (under 0.02 people per km²).
  • Frontier: foragers' or herders' country, below the densest tenth of peopled land (median {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_median')['frontier'],2) }} people per km²).
  • Farmed: farmers' or others' country, below the densest tenth (median {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_median')['farmed'],2) }} people per km²).
  • City hinterland: the densest tenth of peopled land, {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_quantiles_peopled')[1],2) }} people per km² and more (median {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_median')['city hinterland'],2) }}).

Biomes come from the labels the generator gives each cell for its terrain and vegetation (several can apply), read in a fixed order with the first match winning: mountain, tundra, desert, wetland, forest (jungle included), grassland (steppe and open country). The remaining {{ pct(cells('on','0','biome_situation','biome_land_share')['other'],2) }} of land matches none of the six and is left out of the table. Most of it is wild land far from the equator (median latitude {{ sig(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table']['other']['wild']['lat_abs_median'],2) }}°), holding {{ sig(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table']['other']['wild']['per_10000km2'],1) }} dragons per 10,000 km²; the rest is settled lowland and hills without another label.

From the model's present to the 1400s. Three steps.

  1. The model's present is a little later than the game's. The model's present is the renaissance (tech level {{ N['chain']['era_level']['renaissance'] }}); a game set in the 1400s sits at the end of the medieval era (tech level {{ N['chain']['era_level']['medieval'] }}). The kill on adult dragons rises with the square of the tech level, so 1400s peoples would press a little less than the model's present. Denser 1400s settlement would press a little more: the kill grows with people per km², and the overkill of wild herds is already near its full 60% at 0.25 people per km² ({{ pct(N['literature']['pressure_at']['0.25'],3) }} of the way). The two effects go in opposite directions and neither is measured, so the model's present values are taken as they stand.
  2. What the 1400s does change is how much land is in each situation. Wild land shrinks and farmed land and hinterland grow. The table is per area, so it holds; a region's total depends on its mix (Section 7).
  3. The best guess is the model's own value. In round 2 the persecution rule had no source and looked too harsh, so the best guess took a point half-way between the model and the persecution-off arm. That adjustment is retired. The rule now follows the owner's ruling (slight early, rising with tech, never fatal) and has a sourced form (Section 2, step 7), so the table gives the model's value everywhere. Its size is still a judgement: dense renaissance farmers keep 10% of their dragons by construction. The bracket is in Table 7.

What would move these numbers most. Persecution (Section 9, item 1) moves the farmed columns by up to a factor of {{ sig(max(t2['per_10000km2']/t1['per_10000km2'] for bb in ('forest','grassland','desert','tundra','mountain','wetland') for t1, t2 in [(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table'][bb]['farmed'], N['cells']['noper']['0']['biome_situation']['table'][bb]['farmed'])] if t1 and t2 and t1['per_10000km2']),2) }} (Table 7; the city hinterland, densest in people, moves most), and the food channels in hunters' country (item 1b) by more; habitat coverage (item 6) moves wild forest and grassland by up to the food column. Neither the model's own column nor the best guess has been checked against any historical record of large predators under medieval farming; we found none.
