6. The game present: dragons by land and settlement

This section is an extrapolation, not a model result. A typical D&D game sits at about late-medieval technology, the 1400s or so, and the generator's history of its peoples is designed to stop at about that stage by default. It does not reach that far yet: the runs here end with peoples of an earlier stage. The table below takes the model's present-day densities by land type and settlement, then reasons about what the 1400s would change. Every step of that reasoning is stated, and the best-guess column contains a judgement of this review, not the model's.

Situations. Each land cell of every run's present is put in one of four situations:

  • Wild: nobody's country (under 0.02 people per km²).
  • Frontier: foragers' or herders' country, below the densest tenth of peopled land (median {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_median')['frontier'],2) }} people per km²).
  • Farmed: farmers' or others' country, below the densest tenth (median {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_median')['farmed'],2) }} people per km²).
  • City hinterland: the densest tenth of peopled land, {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_quantiles_peopled')[1],2) }} people per km² and more (median {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_median')['city hinterland'],2) }}).

Biomes come from the labels the generator gives each cell for its terrain and vegetation (several can apply), read in a fixed order with the first match winning: mountain, tundra, desert, wetland, forest (jungle included), grassland (steppe and open country). The remaining {{ pct(cells('on','0','biome_situation','biome_land_share')['other'],2) }} of land matches none of the six and is left out of the table. Most of it is wild land far from the equator (median latitude {{ sig(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table']['other']['wild']['lat_abs_median'],2) }}°), holding {{ sig(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table']['other']['wild']['per_10000km2'],1) }} dragons per 10,000 km²; the rest is settled lowland and hills without another label.

From the model's present to the 1400s. Three steps.

  1. Settled land is already at the model's floor. The pressure is 1 − exp(−N/0.05), so it is {{ pct(N['literature']['pressure_at']['0.25'],3) }} of the way at 0.25 people per km². That is true of farmed land and city hinterland everywhere, and of forest, grassland and wetland frontier (at most {{ pct(max(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table'][b]['frontier']['share_under_025_people'] for b in ('forest','grassland','wetland')),2) }} of their cells are thinner than 0.25 people per km²). There denser 1400s settlement cannot press dragons further under the model's rule, and the model's present values carry over unchanged. It is not true of desert, tundra and mountain frontier: {{ pct(min(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table'][b]['frontier']['share_under_025_people'] for b in ('desert','tundra','mountain')),2) }} to {{ pct(max(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table'][b]['frontier']['share_under_025_people'] for b in ('desert','tundra','mountain')),2) }} of their cells are thinner than that (median {{ sig(min(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table'][b]['frontier']['people_km2_median'] for b in ('desert','tundra','mountain')),2) }} to {{ sig(max(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table'][b]['frontier']['people_km2_median'] for b in ('desert','tundra','mountain')),2) }} people per km², mean pressure about {{ sig(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table']['desert']['frontier']['press_mean'],2) }}). Denser 1400s settlement there would lower the model's values further, so for those three the model column is an upper value under its own rule.
  2. What the 1400s does change is how much land is in each situation. Wild land shrinks and farmed land and hinterland grow. The table is per area, so it holds; a region's total depends on its mix (Section 7).
  3. The model's persecution has no source and is, on the evidence of Section 9, too harsh. Ripple et al. find the 17 of the 31 largest carnivores with estimates still hold on average 47% of their historical ranges, and that some persist under much heavier human land use than others (2014, pp. 1241484-1, -6). No paper consulted gives a density for large predators under late-medieval farming. The best guess therefore takes, for frontier and farmed land, the point half-way on a log scale between the model and the persecution-off arm (overkill and hunting kept, persecution removed). It never goes below the model's value: where the persecution-off arm happens to sit lower (a sign of its noise), the model's value is kept. For wild land it takes the model's value (no pressure there), and for city hinterland the model's (persecution there seems least in doubt). This is a judgement; the bracket is in Table 6.

Wild forest holds few: almost no dragon kind lives in forest away from people (only {{ pct(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table']['forest']['wild']['occupied_share'],2) }} of wild forest cells are occupied), though its food would feed {{ bs('off','forest','wild','food_fed_per_10000km2') }} dragons per 10,000 km². Under the model's own rule settled land holds under one dragon per 10,000 km² except desert frontier and farmland ({{ bs('on','desert','frontier','per_10000km2') }} and {{ bs('on','desert','farmed','per_10000km2') }}); the best guess raises frontier and farmed land by roughly the square root of what persecution takes. Of the dragons counted, {{ pct(N['chain']['stage_share'][0]+N['chain']['stage_share'][1],2) }} are wyrmlings and young dragons and {{ pct(N['chain']['stage_share'][3],2) }} ancients (under the default spread of ages; Section 2). A game master wanting a figure for a county of 10,000 km² can read Table 5 directly and expect most of the dragons in it to be young.

What would move these numbers most. The pressure rule (Section 9, item 1) moves the settled columns by up to two orders of magnitude; habitat coverage (item 6) moves wild forest and grassland by up to the food column. Neither the model's own column nor the best guess has been checked against any historical record of large predators under medieval farming; we found none.
