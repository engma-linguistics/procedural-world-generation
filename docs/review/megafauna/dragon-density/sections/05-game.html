6. The game present: dragons by land and settlement

This section is an extrapolation, not a model result. A typical D&D game sits at about late-medieval technology, the 1400s or so, which is where the tech tree will stop by default. The tree does not reach that far yet: the runs end with peoples of an earlier stage. The table below takes the model's present-day densities by land type and settlement, then reasons about what the 1400s would change. Every step of that reasoning is stated, and the best-guess column contains a judgement of this review, not the model's.

Situations. Each land cell of every run's present is put in one of four situations:

  • Wild: nobody's country (under 0.02 people per km²).
  • Frontier: foragers' or herders' country, below the densest tenth of peopled land (median {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_median')['frontier'],2) }} people per km²).
  • Farmed: farmers' or others' country, below the densest tenth (median {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_median')['farmed'],2) }} people per km²).
  • City hinterland: the densest tenth of peopled land, {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_quantiles_peopled')[1],2) }} people per km² and more (median {{ sig(cells('on','0','biome_situation','people_median')['city hinterland'],2) }}).

Biomes are read from each cell's land tags, first match wins: mountain, tundra, desert, wetland, forest (jungle included), grassland (steppe and open country). The remaining {{ pct(cells('on','0','biome_situation','biome_land_share')['other'],2) }} of land (farmland, hills and lowland without another tag) is left out of the table.

From the model's present to the 1400s. Three steps.

  1. Settled land is already at the model's floor. The pressure is 1 − exp(−N/0.05), so it is {{ pct(N['literature']['pressure_at']['0.25'],3) }} of the way at 0.25 people per km², and every settled situation above is denser than that. Denser 1400s settlement cannot press dragons further under the model's rule. The model's present values per km² therefore carry over to the 1400s unchanged for each situation.
  2. What the 1400s does change is how much land is in each situation. Wild land shrinks and farmed land and hinterland grow. The table is per area, so it holds; a region's total depends on its mix (Section 7).
  3. The model's persecution has no source and is, on the evidence of Section 9, too harsh. Ripple et al. find the 17 of the 31 largest carnivores with estimates still hold on average 47% of their historical ranges, and that some persist under much heavier human land use than others (2014, pp. 1241484-1, -6). No paper consulted gives a density for large predators under late-medieval farming. The best guess therefore takes, for frontier and farmed land, the point half-way on a log scale between the model and the persecution-off arm (overkill and hunting kept, persecution removed). For wild land it takes the model's value (no pressure there), and for city hinterland the model's (persecution there seems least in doubt). This is a judgement; the bracket is in Table 6.

What would move these numbers most. The pressure rule (Section 9, item 1) moves the settled columns by up to two orders of magnitude; habitat coverage (item 6) moves wild forest and grassland by up to the food column. Neither the model's own column nor the best guess has been checked against any historical record of large predators under medieval farming; we found none.
