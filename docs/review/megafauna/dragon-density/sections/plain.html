The model works out how many dragons the land can feed. Plants feed grazing herds; a tenth of the herds can be eaten each year; peoples, giants and smaller predators take their part; the dragons get what is left of their share. Where nobody else eats, that comes to about one dragon per {{ sig(N['chain']['one_per_km2_at_1t'],2) }} km² for every tonne of grazers per km². That figure is uncertain by a factor of about ten either way, mostly because nobody knows how much a dragon eats.

Where nobody lives, the model's worlds hold about {{ sig(era('on','0','none'),2) }} dragons per 1,000 km² today (one per {{ sig(1000/era('on','0','none'),2) }} km²), counting every kind and age. That is far fewer than the food allows, because most empty land is poor and the dragon kinds do not live on most of the rich empty land. Where peoples live, dragons are pressed almost out: {{ sig(min(era('on','0',c) for c in ('others','foragers','herders','farmers')),2) }} to {{ sig(max(era('on','0',c) for c in ('others','foragers','herders','farmers')),2) }} per 1,000 km², {{ pct(max(P('0',c,'median') for c in ('others','foragers','herders','farmers')),1) }} or less of what the same land holds when the peoples do not press them. That pressing comes from a hand-set rule with no source behind it.

For a game set in the late Middle Ages, the review's best guess is tens of dragons per 10,000 km² in wild desert and mountains, and no more than a handful in farmed country. In the model as it stands, a region the size of the Sword Coast holds {{ sig(min(sc('0',c,'on') for c in ('others','foragers','herders','farmers')),1) }} to {{ sig(max(sc('0',c,'on') for c in ('others','foragers','herders','farmers')),1) }} dragons of every kind together where peoples live, and about {{ sig(sc('0','none','on'),1) }} where nobody does, mostly young ones. That is too few for each region to be a viable population on its own, so the dragons should be read as one population spread across the world.

The literature says several parts of the model are wrong or weak. The most important: the peoples' pressure needs a real basis; the herd formula is fed the wrong kind of plant growth; and the dragons' food intake is labelled as a reptile's when it behaves like something in between a reptile and a mammal. Section 9 lists twelve changes, each with its evidence and how it would move the numbers.
