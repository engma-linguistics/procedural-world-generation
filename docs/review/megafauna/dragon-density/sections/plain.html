The model works out how many dragons the land can feed. Plants feed grazing herds; the model lets a tenth of the herds be eaten each year; peoples, giants and smaller predators take their part; the dragons get what is left of their share. Where nobody else eats, that comes to about one dragon per {{ sig(N['chain']['one_per_km2_at_1t'],2) }} km² for every tonne of grazers per km². That figure is uncertain by a factor of about ten either way, mostly because nobody knows how much a dragon eats. On grassland it may be lower still, because the herd formula is fed more plant growth than it was built for.

Where nobody lives, the model's worlds hold about {{ sig(era('on','0','none'),2) }} dragons per 1,000 km² today (one per {{ sig(1000/era('on','0','none'),2) }} km²), counting every kind and age. That is fewer than the food allows: most empty land is poor and cold, and dragon kinds live on only {{ pct(cells('on','0','none_now','occupied_share'),2) }} of it. Where peoples live, dragons are pressed almost out: {{ sig(min(era('on','0',c) for c in ('others','foragers','herders','farmers')),2) }} to {{ sig(max(era('on','0',c) for c in ('others','foragers','herders','farmers')),2) }} per 1,000 km², typically 1% to 3% of what the same land holds when the peoples do not press them (up to a quarter in a few runs). That pressing comes from a hand-set rule with no source behind it.

For a game set in the late Middle Ages, the review's best guess is about {{ sig(gpv('grassland','wild'),1) }} dragons per 10,000 km² (a square 100 km on a side) in wild grassland, which in the model's worlds is mostly cold steppe, {{ sig(gpv('desert','wild'),1) }} in wild desert and {{ sig(gpv('mountain','wild'),1) }} in wild mountains, and no more than {{ sig(max(v for b in ('forest','grassland','desert','tundra','mountain','wetland') for v in [gpv(b,'farmed')] if v is not None),1) }} in farmed country. (A few equatorial swamps on two of the six worlds hold far more, about {{ sig(N['cells']['on']['0']['biome_situation']['table']['wetland']['wild']['per_10000km2'],1) }}, but that is two places, not a rule for wetland.) In the model as it stands, a region the size of the Sword Coast holds {{ sig(min(sc('0',c,'on') for c in ('others','foragers','herders','farmers')),1) }} to {{ sig(max(sc('0',c,'on') for c in ('others','foragers','herders','farmers')),1) }} dragons of every kind together where peoples live, and about {{ sig(sc('0','none','on'),1) }} where nobody does, mostly young ones. That is too few for each region to be a viable population on its own, so the dragons should be read as one population spread across the world.

The literature says several parts of the model are wrong or weak. The most important: the peoples' pressure needs a real basis; the herd formula is fed the wrong kind of plant growth; and the dragons' food intake is labelled as a reptile's when it behaves like something in between a reptile and a mammal. Section 9 lists twelve changes, each with its evidence and how it would move the numbers.
