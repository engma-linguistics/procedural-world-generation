4. Methods

Runs. Six worlds, each with four rolls of its history and peoples (7000 to 7003), on a global grid of cells of about {{ f0(N['cell_km2']) }} km² each, with the D&D-style roster of peoples and creatures: {{ len(N['runs']['on']) }} runs with the model as it stands. Each was run a second time with the peoples' pressure switched off, on the same world and roll ({{ len(N['runs']['off']) }} runs). A third arm switches off only the persecution channel and keeps the overkill of wild herds, on the six worlds at roll 7000 ({{ len(N['runs']['noper']) }} runs). Every run used the same version of the model, the one with the persecution rule of Section 2, step 7, and with farmers' numbers following the land's plant growth (Section 4, below). The six worlds are the generator's standard test planets; a roll is an independent draw of the history of peoples and creatures on the same planet.

Two terms used throughout.

  • Pressure off is the second arm: both the overkill of wild herds and the persecution of dragons are switched off everywhere. The peoples' hunters still eat. It is a measuring device, not a world anyone would build.
  • Food-fed density is a per-cell figure: the dragons that the dragons' food share in that cell would feed if dragons lived in every cell and nobody pressed them, i.e. the cell's food for dragons divided by {{ sig(N['chain']['food_t_yr'],2) }} t a year. It is computed in every arm and differs between arms mainly through the herds the overkill removes.

Kinds of country. At each era (31, 29, 25, 10 and 5 kyr ago, kyr meaning thousand years, and the present) each land cell goes in one of five classes. Nobody's country holds fewer than 0.02 people per km², summed over every people. Peopled land goes to foragers', herders' or farmers' country by which way of life holds the most people there; a people's way of life comes from its land-use mix. Land held mostly by peoples who have no named way of life in the tree (dwarves, dragonborn, drow, kobolds, troglodytes, aarakocra, tortles, sea elves, tritons) is others' country. It is easy to overlook: it is about {{ pct(era('on','0','others','land_share'),2) }} of the land now.

Measures. "Dragons per 1,000 km²" is the expected number of dragons of every kind and age in a class's cells over the class's area, pooled over the runs. Cells where no dragon kind lives count as zero, as they would in a survey. For each run the same figure is also computed on its own, giving the run-by-run spread. The paired ratio for a class is (dragons per 1,000 km² with the pressure) ÷ (the same, pressure off) for one world and roll; we give the median and range of the 24 ratios. Switching the pressure off changes the peoples too (their land use reads the herds), sometimes a lot, so a class's area differs between the arms of a pair: now, a peopled class's area with the pressure is {{ pct(min(P('0',c,'area_on_over_off_min') for c in ('others','foragers','herders','farmers')),2) }} to {{ pct(max(P('0',c,'area_on_over_off_max') for c in ('others','foragers','herders','farmers')),2) }} of its area without it, run by run (herders' country, the smallest class, moves most). The ratios therefore compare densities, not counts.

How many people. A people's density in a cell is its way of life's number × a factor for how productive the land is, measured against fixed yardsticks, so a poorer planet holds fewer people. Foragers read the land's wild food, herders the growth of grass and browse, and farmers (new in this version) the total plant growth (net primary production). The factor is (land ÷ yardstick){{ N['chain']['people_exp'] }}, a mildly bent curve. Poor land holds fewer people but is not empty: half as productive land holds {{ pct(0.5**N['chain']['people_exp'],2) }} as many people. The yardsticks ({{ N['chain']['forage_ref'] }} for wild food on a 0-to-5 score, {{ f0(N['chain']['graze_ref']) }} and {{ f0(N['chain']['npp_ref']) }} g of carbon per m² a year for grazing and total growth) were set so that each way's average density over this sweep's land stays at its number. They are not measurements.

Occupancy. A cell is occupied when at least one dragon kind lives there in the model (its habitat suits the kind and the population passes the model's viability test: a check that the kind's population, counting pockets linked by flight, is large enough to last; Section 7). Where nobody lives, a cell's density comes near its food-fed density if the cell is occupied and is zero if not; the occupied share is reported with each table (Section 5.3).

The steps over time. Each run's planet-wide count was read from its time series on either side of 30 kyr ago (where the run hands over from its ice-age pass to its finer recent pass) and of 300 kyr ago (where the present plates begin), in both arms.

The literature lines in Section 5.4 are evaluated at the dragons' ecological mass, {{ sig(N['chain']['eco_mass_kg']/1000,3) }} t, with each paper's own units; the arithmetic is ours.
