## PW-361: Earth's start on the true bed
- **Start.** No grounded cell fails the cell-mean flotation test. Of the 1136 ice cells, 435 sit on beds below the sea, the deepest at −1666 m (L6) and −2308 m (L7). Antarctica holds 25.15 Mkm³ (56.3 m above flotation) and Greenland 2.85 Mkm³ (6.95 m).
- **Deep-water rule.** It calves grounded ice standing in water deeper than `calve_depth`, even with no open sea beside it. At 1.5 km, the 2 deepest cells at L6 (9 at L7) calved every step under ~3 km of ice. Their neighbours then became marine fronts.
  - L7, 4 kyr, orbit fixed: Antarctica lost 0.7 m of sea-level equivalent before the change and gains 0.1 m after.
  - The new threshold is 3 km. Earth's deepest grounded beds are ~2.5 km (the Bentley Subglacial Trench), and PW-199's unresolved basins are ~4 km.
  - New test: `test_deep_grounded_ice_stands`.
- **Unforced retreat** (`obliquity_amp=0 ecc_mean=0 ecc_100=0 ecc_400=0 orbit_ref=start`, 20 kyr):
  - Antarctica loses 95 of its 1003 cells, all marine (median bed −670 m, Amundsen and Siple coasts): 2.3 Mkm³, or −3.8 m above flotation. It then holds ~22.1–22.4 Mkm³ for a whole 1 Myr zoom.
  - A front ~670 m deep needs ~670 m/yr of inflow to stand. The shallow-ice flow at 110 km cells delivers ~100–200 m/yr.
  - Unbuttressed, a marine sheet on a retrograde bed is unstable. The real sheet stands because its shelves hold it back.
  - Greenland's region grows by 2.3 m of sea-level equivalent (77 new cells, 37 of them on the shelf), so the sea level stays near 0 by cancellation.
  - Options are in the draft (lean: cold-ocean buttressing).
- **Cycle (1 Myr, default).** Before the batch the sea level ran from −15 to +14 m, and Greenland flipped between 0 and 3.8 Mkm³ at most precession maxima. Now it runs from −24 to +9 m, and Greenland shrinks only in the strongest interglacials: 0.9 Mkm³ at 788 kyr, ~2.1 at 228 and 708.

## PW-114: the orbital response over ice
`orbit_anomaly` and the time zoom's year built the mixed response the same way. They smoothed the absorbed share (1 on the sea, 0.2 on bright ice) over 600 km, then scaled it by the land's `insol_k`. The open sea around Greenland therefore counted as land absorbing in full, although the sea's own air takes only 0.3 of the land's response.

The new `timezoom.orbit_k`:
- bare land keeps `insol_k`, and the sea keeps its own 0.3 share;
- bright ice takes the air mass's mix of each surface's own response (land 1, sea 0.3, ice 0.2);
- partly bright cells blend the two.

Greenland's ice now feels 0.35 of the land's response (was 0.70), and Antarctica's 0.23 (was 0.42). New test: `test_orbit_response_mixes_each_surface_own`.

Eemian-like start, sea-level equivalent change (m), before → after:

| kyr | Greenland | Antarctica | total |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | +7.0 → +3.0 | +1.4 → +0.6 | +8.2 → +3.6 |
| 4 | gone → +6.0 | → +1.8 | +10.4 → +8.0 |
| 8 | gone → +5.2 | → +4.1 | +13.2 → +9.2 |
| 20 | Greenland back to 1.54 → 1.85 Mkm³ | | +4.7 → +3.1 |

The Antarctic share is mostly the unforced marine retreat above; the Eemian orbit adds little to it. Greenland is still ~2.5× too sensitive, for two reasons:
- **Cold bias.** Its model summer on the ice is 4.3 °C colder than observed, and the `obs` anchor only cools.
- **Resolution.** At L6 (133 cells) it has no cold high interior: the observed cell-mean summer on its thickest cells is −7 °C, against ~−12 °C at the summit.

I tried two alternatives and rejected both:
- **A two-way `obs` anchor** (warming Greenland to its observed summer). Greenland still grows when unforced, collapses in 2 kyr under the Eemian orbit, and, before PW-035, never came back.
- **Mixing bare land's response too** (maritime coasts). It is sound in itself, but Greenland then never shrinks in 1 Myr, and the Earth cycle flattens to −32..−2 m with North American ice that never leaves. It would also change bare ground's response, which the run's new-cap test shares.

The run's cycle-averaged balance (`earth.ice_sheets`) still uses the old mix: draft `draft-run-ice-orbit-mix-sea.md`, a one-line change in the run's lane. Its effect on the drift sweep is small: median −138 m with the old mix, −147 m with the new.

## PW-035: the start anchor follows the ice
The anchor was a fixed cooling of each anchored cell (the start ice plus 3 rings) for the whole zoom. It is now `anchor × clip(ice / snow_h, 0, 1)` in `climate_step`, and the same in `finish.orbit_dt`.
- It leaves with the ice, returns where the ice readvances, and no longer cools ring cells before any ice reaches them.
- New test `test_start_anchor_leaves_with_the_ice`: bare cells had 3.2 °C of anchor left before the change and keep none now.
- Earth is unchanged to 0.1 m over 1 Myr: its anchored cells that lose their ice are West Antarctica's marine ones, which turn to sea. Runs use `anchor=none`.

## PW-295 and PW-241: the drift from a run's moment
**Sweep.** 150 kyr zooms from 200, 400, 600, 800 and 1000 Myr of L5 seeds 2 and 11 (in-memory simulations, `tzmulti.py`). End sea level: median −147 m, worst −275 m, 5 of 10 below −150 m (before the batch: −137, −284, 5). The cold moments drive it:
- seed 11 @ 200 Myr: −200 m at the end, −349 at worst;
- seed 2 @ 600 Myr: −274 m.

The milder moments drift slowly with the cycles (seed 2 @ 800 Myr, dT +2.2: ice 0.4 → 6.6%, −249 m).

**Pure consistency** (orbit, CO₂ and ice albedo off) from seed 11 @ 200 Myr (dT −8, 12.6% ice, 110.7 Mkm³): −72 m in 2 kyr, −282 m by 20 kyr, then flat. After 20 kyr:
- the old ice holds +53 Mkm³ more (median +690 m; the margin cells go from 340 to 1730 m, the next cells in from 1530 to 2360 m);
- 427 new land cells hold +44 Mkm³. They reach up to 8 cells beyond the run's margin, and their interiors gain +0.8 m/yr under 2.8 km of ice, where an edge's 330 m would lose ~8 m/yr;
- 154 new shelf cells hold +8 Mkm³.

**Causes:**
1. **The run's profile is thin.** `earth.sheet_surface` uses a 50 kPa Nye profile and takes each cell's rise at its inner edge.
   - On Earth's real extent and bed it gives Antarctica 19.8 Mkm³ at L6 (ETOPO1: 25.1; median −415 m) and 19.2 at L7.
   - 75 kPa gives 25.4 / 24.9; 100 kPa gives 29.6 / 29.2. Real sheets fit ~70–100 kPa.
   - The cell-mean rise adds ~11% at L6 and less at L7.
2. **The run leaves out the height feedback.** The run tests new cells under an edge's ice. Once the time zoom's flow has thickened them, they accumulate.

**Scratch test.** I monkeypatched the run's last climate update in a script; nothing was merged. Seed 11 @ 200 Myr, no forcing:

| run's rule | run ice (Mkm³) | 20 kyr drift | 150 kyr default, end |
|---|---|---|---|
| current (50 kPa, edge-sampled) | 110.7 | −282 m | −200 m |
| 100 kPa | 144 | −174 m | −111 m |
| 100 kPa + cell-mean rise | 171 | −92 m | −42 m |

Limiting the ablation zone to cells that lose under 1 m/yr changed nothing (−281 m). On L6 seed 3199 @ 736 Myr (now a mild moment: dT −1.8, 1.5% ice), the drift is −35 m unforced and −114 m in 150 kyr with the cycles. With 75 kPa and the cell mean it is −27 and −105: the drift there is extent growth, not the profile.

**Other things I checked:**
- The time zoom's own driving stresses are Earth-like (steepest edge per cell: median 37–41 kPa against Earth's 45–49). Its margins aren't overly steep, so this is not a time-zoom flow artefact.
- Lowering the diffusivity cap makes the drift worse: −449 m at `d_cap` 1e8, −282 at 3e8, −236 at 1e9.

Why I didn't fix it here:
- Both causes are in `planet/tectonics/earth.py` (another lane).
- `icemass.EDGE_H` is set from the same 50 kPa.
- The fix makes the run's sheets ~25–50% heavier, which deepens the cold eras that PW-240 already finds too deep.

Draft `draft-run-ice-profile-thin.md` gives the options (lean: 75 kPa plus the cell mean, together with PW-240).
