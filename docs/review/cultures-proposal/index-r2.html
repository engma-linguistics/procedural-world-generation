In plain words

Nothing about the shape of the plan changes. A culture is still the unit; each gets a lect (a way of speaking), a name stack, and, later, a ruler, attitudes and gods. What changed is what we promise and in what order.

  • Language is fuzzy and partly political. "A language" is a stretch of mutually understandable lects, not a box. Where the lines fall depends on politics too: a ruling lect has prestige (your Norman example), so a conquered people's speech bends toward it. You will be able to click a people and see a strong-colour blob that fades with distance: "the language of these people", with hard cutoffs where something wholly different is spoken.
  • Time and depth. The in-world horizon is 10 kyr and we stop tracking anything deeper. Language can begin more than once, at about the same time across species, but not tied to when a species became genetically modern.
  • Names. Rivers and mountains keep their names through conquests; settlements and states change theirs. Outsiders lump distinct small cultures into one; the viewer will show both views.
  • War is mostly a change of owner. A battle ends when the loser has lost perhaps 10-20% (your figure, not a sourced one), and the farmers just have new landlords, unless the winners are intolerant of their species or culture. The same tolerance decides whether a state keeps other peoples' records (your cuneiform point).
  • Writing is shaped by what it is written on. Secret dragon writing is parked as an optional scenario.
  • Gods are only placeholder domains in v0. The religion design comes after a talk with you; nothing real before it.
  • Dragons and kin. Dragon kinds are several cultures each, but "Draconic" stays one language with colour dialects. Kobolds live under dragons and speak close to them. Dragon dominion becomes dragon realms (polities with subjects). Goblins mostly stay low-tech and get folded into other goblinoids' realms.
  • Order. Languages v0 and polities v0 are output-side (a readout after the run). Polities go into the history in v1, together with authored nudges ("this people rises here") by re-running from a time zoom.
  • Your four questions are answered in section 2, each with a call: de-hardcode groups (yes, in two steps), the history zoom (start now, needed before polities v1), the village experiment (designed, not run), Catalan and Spanish (I could confirm only what the distance table says, not the history).

2. Your open questions, answered

2.1 Should groups be de-hardcoded?

Recommendation: yes, in two steps; keep authored groups as seeds, not constraints.

What a group is today: an authored way of life inside a people (human seafarers, steppe orcs), not cut into demes; its land counts as its people's (round 1). Cultures already carry a way of life and two drifting skills (sea craft, open-country herding), so most of what a group expresses already emerges per culture.

The density ladder (PW-1275) says way of life changes with tech for part of a people. That is the pull the other way: if ways of life are fully per culture, a people that half-farms and half-herds has to be two cultures, which then conflicts with PW-1254 (no merging across ways of life) and inflates the culture count.

Weighing it: - Per-culture way of life only (full de-hardcode): simple, but a way-of-life transition (herders taking up farming) becomes a culture split and an artificial boundary. Rejected. - A share of a culture on each way of life (the ladder's reading): transitions are gradual and cost no splits. A new culture appears only when a share persists, is separated in space, and the PW-1254 rule (no merging across ways of life) keeps it apart. This is the sounder mechanism. - Hardcoded groups: they work, but they stop steppe orcs from emerging where the land says so, and a world with no authored group gets no equivalent.

So: step 1, land the ladder with way-of-life shares per culture (PW-1275 plus the shift-with-tech rule). Step 2, make groups seed an initial share and a starting skill, then check on the dnd runs that the emergent ways of life reproduce what the groups gave; retire the group code path once they do (no backward compatibility, per the repo rules). I have not read the ladder's design beyond the ticket and your ruling; the shape of the shares is a judgement for the lane.

2.3 Villages: tracked or generated? An experiment design (not run)

Your ask: test with and without village tracking, always keep villages for the last ~200 years, and generate villages on demand from town data, possibly rolling up to the city level.

Question the experiment answers: do tracked villages change anything that matters downstream (towns, cities, polity seeds, cultures' sizes, minorities) beyond storage and complexity?

Arms (same world, same seed, L6 first, then one L7 world): - A: baseline. Villages tracked every 100-year step. - B: no villages until the end. No villages tracked before the last 200 years (two steps); the last 200 always include them. - C: on demand. B plus a function that generates a cell's villages when asked, from the cell's town data and the people left over after towns: count and sizes from the same central-place rule, deterministic given the cell and step.

Measures: 1. Town and city counts, sizes and founding steps, compared A against B (do towns appear in the same cells and steps?). 2. Culture sizes, minorities and colonies in the settlement ledger (do they shift?). 3. Polity-relevant seeds: the largest town per culture per step, and chiefdom-hazard inputs. 4. For C: the generated villages in the last 200 years against the tracked ones (count and size distributions per cell). 5. Cost: ledger size on disk and wall time for the settlements pass, per arm.

Pass criteria (suggested, yours to change): B's towns and cities within the A run-to-run variation of a rerun with a different seed; C's last-200-year villages within the same variation of the tracked ones.

Cost: two world runs per arm at L6 (peoples history already exists; the settlements pass is output-side), plus one small code change: a track_villages switch and the on-demand generator for arm C. I have not measured the settlements pass: the cultures pass is about 2 s a run (round 1), and I expect settlements to be seconds to minutes, but that is a guess, so the experiment's first step is timing arm A. Size: one Sonnet lane, small; roughly the same again for the L7 repeat. It does not touch the history. Do it before polities v0 builds on the ledger, since polities read what the ledger holds.

2.4 Catalan and Spanish: contact or normalisation?

You expect they did have contact but not direct communication and normalisation for a long time. What I can actually say from what was read:

  • Wichmann (2019) puts Catalan-Spanish at LDN 0.66, the highest of his example pairs (table above), well past his language/dialect cut-off at LDN 0.51 (round 1 full read). So they count as clearly separate languages by that measure, and more different than Cairo and Moroccan Arabic (0.58).
  • In the round 1 model, a split with no contact reaches the cut-off at about 1.27 kyr. Converting 0.66 the same way (LDND about 0.745, s about 0.255) gives about 2.0 kyr of no-contact divergence, which I computed from Holman's fit and Wichmann's conversion. That is a model equivalent, not a date: contact would lower the similarity pull and make the real time longer.
  • I did not read anything on the actual Iberian history, so I cannot confirm or refute "different enough and far enough away for a long time". What the model offers is the distinction you are drawing: contact (borrowing, levelling) raises surface similarity, while normalisation under a standard or a ruling lect raises exposure, which raises intelligibility without touching the surface similarity (section 3.1). The literature says exposure dominates real intelligibility (r 0.87-0.93, Gooskens & van Heuven 2020). So the sim can tell "close because they talked" from "close because one was the standard".
  • A suggestion if you want it confirmed: a cheap targeted read of the Catalan-Spanish history, as a Haiku pass, before we tune the contact term on it. I would not use this pair as a calibration target until then.

3. The proposal, revised

3.1 Languages (Stage 0, no words, no sounds)

Stands from round 1: one lect per culture, born, split and ended with it; the true lect tree is deep as the demes (300 kyr), tried first; "languages" are derived clusters of lects, never stored.

Changed: - Fuzzy, partly political. A language is a stretch of lects with high mutual intelligibility (MI). The line is drawn at the Wichmann cut-off (s about 0.40) as the default, but the viewer's language blob and the brief both read MI, and politics shifts MI through exposure: a ruling lect, a written standard or a trade speech raises how well subjects understand it. So "the language count" may depend on politics (your L2 ruling: mostly A, with politics). Both mismatches are visible per culture: one language called two things because of culture, and distinct cultures with indistinguishable lects (your China and Hindi-Urdu cases; the China example is yours, I did not source it). - The 10 kyr horizon. In-world scholars can see relations up to about 10 kyr back. Beyond that nothing is tracked or flagged: there is no beyond_horizon field and no per-family horizon. Families are clades whose root is younger than 10 kyr, a constant. The true tree keeps whatever the demes give; we do not ask what the truth was beyond what scholars could know. Splits, merges and overlaps just happen. - Language can begin more than once. Each people's stem draws its own onset of spoken language from a window around a shared date, near-simultaneous across species, and set apart from its genetic modernity (the humans' 200 kyr of language against a longer genetic modernity is your example, a rough figure from your note). Inside one stem, separate demes that have not met can have started separately too: a lect root can appear in more than one place, and contact spreads the faculty after (PW-947). The window's width and the lag after modernity are my judgement; I'd start with a window of a few tens of kyr and flag it for tuning. No pair across stems is related (s_inherited = 0). - Cultures split before languages. T_APART_KYR 0.15-0.6 kyr, a no-contact split at 0.6 kyr has s about 0.62, still dialects (round 1). Expect many cultures per language, as on Earth. PW-1254's rules (cohesion by lifespan; no merging across ways of life) apply: two cultures of the same people with different ways of life stay two cultures, usually one language.

The model, trimmed (stands): - Similarity s between two lects (ASJP-style, 1 minus LDND; Holman 2011). Divergence with no contact: s(t) = s0 * r^(2t), s0 = 0.92, r = 0.72 per kyr; each lineage has a lognormal multiplier rate_x (sigma about 0.3, the fit's 29% scatter). - Contact pulls the surface back: s' = s + [ -(la+lb) s + kappa c_ab (s0 - s) ] dt. Neighbours along a chain stay close; ends diverge: that is the dialect continuum. Two numbers per pair: s_inherited (the comparative method; never pulled back) and s_surface (what a listener hears). - MI: mi_inherent = f(s_surface), a logistic curve of ours, fitted to European close relatives (no published closed form; Gooskens & van Heuven 2020 give only correlations). Then mi_ab = mi_inherent + (1 - mi_inherent) E_ab, with exposure E_ab (judgement) growing with b's speakers dominating shared cells, b's culture ruling a's (polities), or b being a written standard or trade speech. A small seeded asymmetry stands in for the Portuguese-Spanish structural residue. - Bands in the brief: dialects (mi >= about 0.7), partly understood (0.4-0.7; 0.4 is Gooskens et al.'s workable threshold), separate (< 0.4). - Pairs: touching demes now plus each lect's nearest sister; any other pair on demand. - Light events: split, koine (sister cultures merged), shift (one culture swallowed), death, contact episodes, standard. - Loan strata (forms empty): core, cultural, food (every crop or animal spread writes a loan with its route), substrate (from shifts). Sizes from the World Loanword Database: mean borrowed share about 25%; by field social and political 0.64, religion 0.49, agriculture 0.45, food and drink 0.37, kinship 0.23, body 0.17; traded goods (olive 0.93, wine 0.74) borrow heavily, basic states (raw 0.05, cooked 0.06) stay native. - Checks, not targets: two lects about 1.3 kyr apart with no contact sit at the line; a walker people's open range shows a chain whose ends are below the line while neighbours stay above; similarity against travel distance looks like Seguy's curve with scatter; lect density follows growing season (Nettle 1998; trend, not constant).

What politics adds (changed): the ruling lect's prestige drives shift among a state's subjects (Abrams & Strogatz, in 3.3), and raises E_ab toward the ruling lect. Your Norman-conquest example is the intended picture: a court language that does not replace the commoners' speech, but pulls a large share of its vocabulary and prestige register.

3.2 Names

Stands: the name stack per culture with forms empty, glosses only (your E/X/P taxonomy): endonym pattern and gloss, glossonym, an exonym per neighbour that matters from the relationship, scholarly names for dead cultures, name history. Gloss strings fix PW-1249's repeated placeholders. English glosses and forms empty: settled.

Changed: - Some names are very resilient. Names of rivers and mountains persist through conquests and language shifts (your note); settlement names change more often; polity names change most. Implementation: each place name carries a persistence class (water and mountain features high, settlements medium, polity low) and a small chance of an arbitrary "sticks forever" name regardless of class. The classes and rates are my judgement; I have not read the toponymy literature this round. The substrate stratum from shifts is where these names are recorded. - Outsiders lump. A region's several small, distinct cultures can be one exonym to a distant observer, and may look alike on the map, while each regards the others as distinct (your Native American example). The culture split already makes them distinct (local, by time apart). Lumping is observer-side: reputation.py already lumps by observer and subject (round 1); extend it so the lump size grows with distance and thin contact. Store both views and let the viewer switch (section 5).

3.3 Polities

Stands (round 1 2.1, trimmed): - Tiers: band or tribe (no polity), chiefdom, state, empire (emergent: rules more than one culture or people). - Output-side v0, using the settlement ledger, techs, cultures and circumscription (counter._circumscribed); in the history in v1. - Chiefdoms start where a culture with chiefdoms and towns has a hazard of founding one at its largest town, scaled by local density, resource concentration, a contagion term and circumscription (Carneiro 2012). - Reach: a chiefdom about half a day's travel (25-30 km on foot, Spencer 2010), a state 50-250 km soon after state institutions appear; writing, law, roads, horses and ships extend that, by judgement. - Frontiers breed empires (Turchin 2009, Turchin et al. 2013); polities end with a base hazard (Arbesman 2011, exponential, mean about 220 years) and tiers move one step at a time (Currie et al. 2010, abstract only). - Size checks, not caps: Taagepera 1997.

Changed: - Chiefdom and grid. By your follow-up, a chiefdom is a one-cell thing at L7 (a third to a fifth of a cell, from the round 1 figure); at coarser cells it is smaller still. It is a tier label on a town and its cell, not a map shape. - Gate (P2, nearly B). A chiefdom needs writing; a small state can barely do without it. I read your "impetus" point as a feedback: need for records pressures the adoption of writing, so writing's invention hazard gets a bump where a chiefdom or small state forms. Output-side v0 cannot feed back (the tech tree is output only, PW-946), so v0 just uses the gate with a slight lenience (a chiefdom without writing is allowed if its neighbours have it, because it can borrow it). The real feedback arrives with the history in v1. - War is mostly a change of owner (P3 B, not v0). The losing side's army breaks at perhaps 10-20% losses (your figure; I did not source it), and the cells change hands. A cell's population does not change much: the farmers keep farming under a new ruler. Population loss and displacement depend on the winner's tolerance of the subject's species or culture (attitudes, 3.4): tolerant winners cost little, intolerant ones expel, enslave or exterminate a share. This agrees with round 1's finding that war deaths at sourced violent-death rates were 0-0.2% of any people (lane 101) and with your "trim, not topple" expectation, except in long sustained aggression. P3 B (war deaths and displacement) is eventual, not v0; v0 draws ownership only. - Pulling out placeholder formulae. The in-fighting term (FEUD_CUT, SOCIAL_Q) and the state-pacification proxy G and hostile-frontier share F in counter.py are placeholders for what polities replace. A prerequisite lane lists and isolates them (section 7) so polities can swap them cleanly (PW-1258). - Languages (the Norman point). The ruling lect's prestige drives shift among its subjects: round 1 used Abrams & Strogatz (2003): dx/dt = y c x^a s - x c y^a (1-s), a = 1.31 +/- 0.25, status s from tier, writing and cities. The shift is computed per cell per 100-year step, and an elite lect can add vocabulary without displacing the commoners' (partial shift, a large loan stratum). Political complexity predicts a language's area (Currie & Mace 2009). - Tolerance and records. A tolerant, record-keeping empire preserves other cultures' records (your cuneiform point, which I accept as plausible but did not source). So the survival of a conquered people's texts and scholarly names = writing x the state's tolerance toward them, not just writing. - Population from the density ladder. A polity's population within reach reads one accessor for density. Until PW-1275 lands that accessor reads today's density; when the ladder lands (way of life x tech stage x land productivity, kind factor 0.8-1.2) it swaps in with no change to polities. - Dragon realms. Dragon dominion over land belongs in polities, not in habitat learning (your ruling): a dragon realm is a polity whose ruling culture is a dragon culture, with subjects of other peoples (kobolds, dragonborn, others). Its reach is the flight range, not the tech tiers. Kobolds gravitate to dragons and occasionally form dragon, dragonborn and kobold realms. Open decision O4 asks how such a realm's gate works. - Goblins. Goblins mostly stay low on the tech tree (short brutish lives), so they rarely form polities of their own and more often become subjects of other goblinoids (hobgoblins, bugbears) via the same ruling-lect shift. Their lects stay separate unless shifted. - Emergent first; authored nudges later. Polities emerge by default. In v1 you can select in a time zoom "this specific people rises about now" or "these are wiped out or nearly out by a neighbour" and the sim re-runs from just before to contrive it. That needs the history zoom (2.2) and the polities in the history. The round 1 [[event]] kind = "polity" back-fill stays as the fallback for Seven Meals empires (PW-785). - Leader share (P4). No polity knobs aimed at it; D31 A stands, report the share after polities and stop.

3.5 Writing

Stands: writing is a tech (tokens, proto-writing, writing floor 5.3 kyr, alphabet); effects output-side: standards, scholarly names, literacy share, memory of conquests.

Changed: - Media shape scripts. A script lineage carries a medium (clay, reed or papyrus, bark or leaf, stone or wood as runes, hide), read from the land and techs, not from a date. Real scripts were arguably shaped by their media (your note: runes, cuneiform in clay, papyrus; leaves feel "a bit more spurious but decent enough"). v0 stores only the medium id and the lineage; the shape effects are a later, deeper design ("way WAY in the reeds"). - Record survival. Writing x tolerance (3.3). A medium also changes survival of records (clay lasts, leaves rot), which the same field enables later. - Secret dragon writing (optional scenario, later). If dragons reach behavioural modernity far earlier than others, writing could be a secret they keep for a very long time and that eventually leaks, against dragons simply discovering their own writing or being gifted it. It should be a scenario switch on the tech tree's contact gate, not a default. Not in v0 or v1.

3.6 Dragons, kobolds and kin

  • Dragon kinds are one to five cultures each (D36 B). The round 1 line "cohesion already makes one culture per colour" is out of date.
  • "Draconic" is one language across colours (settled), with colour dialects (MI about 0.8, round 1's lean) held together by dragon cohesion (0.9, a round 1 figure for dragons). One lect per dragon culture.
  • Kobolds start from Draconic at their split (round 1 L5 A, which you approved with the note that kobolds closely align themselves to life under dragons). Their lect stays close to the dragons' by exposure (high E_ab toward the dragon lect when under a dragon realm) and drifts only slowly.
  • Dragon dominion is a polity (3.3); goblins are subjects or low-tech (3.3).
  • Dragons are sapient: nowhere do the models treat their speech as a lesser thing.
