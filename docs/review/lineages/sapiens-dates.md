# Candidate dates for each stem's sapiens node

For the owner's gut call (PW-971, with PW-965, PW-947, PW-945). Nothing here is a verdict. Dates are "before present". Fantasy stems have no ground truth, so each section says which parts are fact and which are analogy.

## What is being dated

A stem's `sapient = "T"` marks when minds arrive for the whole stem. Rulings of 10-28 that frame the choice:

- Language comes only after the stem's sapiens node.
- Only bare-bones tool use may precede it, as the literature hints.
- On these timescales almost any contact spreads language to any genetically modern species, so a node does not need to be synchronised with its neighbours.
- Dragons and the other elders are older.
- The chromatic/metallic split is about 2.5 Myr.
- Peoples split after the node (a constraint of the model, not of nature).

**What this says, as fact.**

- Our own tool record runs about 3 Myr from first stone flaking (Lomekwi, 3.3 Myr) to the first sapiens fossils (about 300 kyr). Tools for most of that time were simple: Oldowan flakes and choppers, then Acheulean handaxes. The literature does not attach minds, in the sense of language and culture, to any of those tools by consensus.
- Behavioural modernity markers (ochre, beads) appear 100-75 kyr, but they lag the first sapiens fossils by 200 kyr or more. The record of symbols is thin and depends on what survives. A modern body does not date a modern mind.
- Control of fire is a rough 1 Myr to 790 kyr signal and regular use is argued to be later; fire is not an early anchor and I would not use it for a node.

**The Neanderthal question.** Dediu & Levinson argue that Neanderthals and Denisovans likely had language, partly because the shared capacities would predate the split between those lineages and ours, which they put at well over 500 kyr (my recollection of the paper; the split date is unverified). If so, the "minds" node for a group of peoples is the last common ancestor of all of them, not the first fossil of the best-known member. For the model, that is the right reading: the node sits before the stem's first split, which is already the requirement. It also says the real analogue of our own node is probably 500-800 kyr, older than Irhoud. A counter-view exists (language arrived late and abruptly in sapiens only; Everaert et al. 2017, Trends Cogn Sci 21:569-571, doi:10.1016/j.tics.2017.05.007 is a short statement of that school, cited for the existence of the position and not read in full). The owner's ruling on spread by contact makes this dispute mostly harmless for the model.

**FOXP2 caveats.** FOXP2 is a speech-and-language gene, not "the language gene". Enard et al. reported two human-specific amino acid changes under selection; Krause et al. found the same derived variant in Neanderthals; Atkinson et al. 2018 found no evidence for a recent selective sweep, which undercuts a late, sapiens-only origin. Net: do not hang a date on FOXP2. It is a weak constraint, and it leans slightly toward older.

**Animal tool use as a baseline.** Chimpanzees, capuchins and New Caledonian crows use or make tools with no language and no culture of the human kind. Proffitt et al. show wild capuchins making flakes that look like archaeological stone tools. Weir et al. show crows bending wire into hooks. So for the avian stem, a long run of bare-bones tool use is not just allowed but expected; the stem could start with it. Bare-bones tool use also fits the ruling: it precedes the node, and it is not evidence of a mind.

**Deep-time cognition of birds and cats.** I did not find a verified source that puts a date on the origin of corvid-level cognition or on big-cat cognition; both are mostly comparative studies of living species. Clayton & Emery is a verified primer on corvid cognition in living birds. Anything about brain size scaling in birds (neuron density) or about cats is unverified here; see the wishlist. The honest statement is that the record says nothing about when either lineage's minds would arrive.

## 2. The scaling argument for fantasy stems

All of this is analogy. Three ways to place a node, each with a flaw.

**A. Copy the hominin gap.** About 3 Myr from first tools to sapiens, then about 300 kyr of sapiens. Each stem's node sits between the stem start and the first split, a gap of 0.5 to 17 Myr (a human-sized 3 Myr would not fit the hominid stem at all: it is 2 Myr long and has only 0.5 Myr before the first split). So hominids have to be compressed against the real record, and I read this as a sign that the model's hominid stem is not a literal copy of ours. Lesson: ignore the 3 Myr; treat the real record as "tools long before minds" in shape only.

**B. Scale by generation time.** Humans have a generation of about 25-29 years (the toml uses 25 as default, from Fenner 2005, as quoted in the file). Three million years is about 110,000 generations from Lomekwi to now; 300 kyr is about 11,000 generations since the first sapiens. The user brief's "10-12k generations from Homo to sapiens" matches the second figure, sapiens to now, not Homo to sapiens (that is nearer 100,000).

For dragons at about 430 years per generation (the default under `meta.dragon_generation = "schedule"`):

- 11,000 generations is 4.7 Myr. A dragon mind that is as old, in generations, as ours would be about 4.7 Myr old. The current 4 Myr is therefore close to a generation-matched date.
- 110,000 generations is 47 Myr, longer than the stem (19.5 Myr, about 45,000 generations in all). Scaling the whole hominin gap by generations says dragons never get minds. So the full scaling is silly, and I would not use it for the long arm; the short arm (sapiens age in generations) is defensible only as one way to bound "late".

Generation scaling is also a poor fit for evolution in itself: evolutionary change per year depends on population size, selection and mutation supply as much as on generation count, and big long-lived animals with tiny populations change slowly per generation too. Treat it as a way to get a number to argue with.

**C. Fit the in-world constraints.** The node must precede the first split, and the owner wants the elders older. This gives a window per stem. Within it, a larger window with a lot of room before the first split produces a longer "mindful but not yet divided" interval, in which a people has language, without yet splitting into kinds; in the model, that interval is where the stem is a single people with one language family (PW-947). Too short and the first split happens almost at the node, which is fine as drama but leaves no room for early culture.

## 4. Open questions for the owner

1. Should the elders (dragons) be far older than everyone else's nodes, or is "older than the first split by a good margin" enough? The lean above (5 Myr) is about two to four times older than the others' but not geologically separate.
2. Should the node mean "the last common ancestor of all peoples in the stem" (the Dediu reading, older than any fossil) or "when the first mind appears" (younger)? The model's constraint already implies the first; this affects whether we write dates that sound like a first-fossil date.
3. Should goblinoids sit near hominids? Contact spreads language anyway, so it is a flavour question; the sample puts them 800 kyr apart.
4. Do cats deserve a node as old as the hominid one, given no tool use to point at, or should it be later, with leonin and tabaxi splitting soon after?
5. Should the avian stem's long tool-using run before the node appear in the Tree tab as a labelled stage, or is the sapiens marker enough?
6. Does the fantasy hominid stem need to match the real timeline at all? The real gap of about 3 Myr does not fit its 2 Myr window.

### Tech gating (techtree.py, techtree.toml)

- Under a stem with a `sapient` node, only techs marked `bare = true` arise, spread, or are inherited before the node: `knapping` and `handaxes` (Lomekwi/Oldowan, Acheulean). Everything else waits, gesture and call systems included (my choice: they are language's root, and the bare set stays tools only). Kits are not gated, so a dragon still has gesture and innate magic from its start, as body; dragons have no hands, so they get no bare tools and keep their own path.
- Language now spreads by contact (`modern_contact`, spread 1.0) to any people under a stem with a node, bringing gesture with it. Peoples with no stem node above them (arrivals, test fixtures) behave as before: language is their own.
- Dragons still cannot invent language before 2 Myr (`elder_kyr = 2000`, unchanged, a dating from the old file): so their mind stands for ~3 Myr before language. This matches the owner's "millions of years of a mind that never built tech" and was left.
