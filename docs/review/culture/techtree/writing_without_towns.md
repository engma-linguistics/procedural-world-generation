# Writing without towns: real-world counterexamples (D62, PW-1495; lane 208)

Daniel's question (D62): "Some level of organization at a societal level feels reasonable as a prerequisite for writing. Are there
real-world counterexamples?" He ruled A: no town, no invention of state techs. This page checks that against the record, and
section 4 checks the model's arm S against it.

Every claim below cites a page fetched by the lane (2026-11-07). Where only a search snippet was seen, it says so. Most of these
pages are Wikipedia summaries. They are good enough for "who had towns and chiefs", but not for fine dating.

## In plain words

- **Inventing writing: Daniel's instinct holds.**
  - The four accepted independent inventions all came from states or near-states: Sumer (Uruk, a city), Egypt (a proto-state elite
    just before unification), Shang China (a kingdom), and Mesoamerica (Zapotec and Maya chiefdoms becoming states).
  - The only serious exception is **rongorongo** on Easter Island. That was a chiefdom: no town and no state, but ruling families and
    priests. Rongorongo may be an independent invention from the 1400s, but this is not settled.
  - **Village sign systems** (Vinča in the Balkans, Jiahu in China) are mostly not counted as writing.
  - **Scripts invented by individuals** (Cherokee, Vai, Yup'ik) were all made by people who had seen writing. They were inventions
    under contact, not from nothing. The Cherokee also had a national council by then.
  - So "no town, no invention" is close to the record. A chiefdom inventing writing (rongorongo) is the rare edge case.
- **Taking writing up: many peoples without towns did.**
  - **Germanic runes** were used by peoples with no towns in Scandinavia, under chiefs. **Ogham** was used in Ireland, which had petty
    kings and no towns. Neither fact is stated on the fetched pages; see the caveats in section 3.
  - **Tifinagh** is used by the **Tuareg**, pastoral nomads organised in confederations under a supreme chief.
  - The **Old Turkic** script belonged to steppe khanates. These are states, but nomadic ones.
  - Some **village peoples in the Philippines and Sumatra** with no towns and no state wrote for love songs and letters, at high
    rates: the Hanunó'o Mangyan, the Lampong and the Batak.
  - Uses were small: names on graves, ownership marks, magic, love poems. There were no archives, laws or tax rolls.
- **Peoples with no chiefs at all:** no clean case was found of an egalitarian forager or herder band taking up writing. The Mangyan
  are the nearest candidate, but the lane could not source whether they have chiefs.
- **For the model:** Daniel's D60 B wording, "towns or chiefdoms of its own", fits the record for taking writing up.
  - But the model's chiefdoms *tech* is near-universal: 96-100% of peoples hold it by 500 years ago.
  - Reading it would let 89-90% of the peoples without towns write, foragers and herders included.
  - So the change is not built. A draft offers "a chief seat of its own" instead (section 4, [round4.md](round4.md)).

## 2. Candidate exceptions to "inventing needs a state"

- **Rongorongo (Rapa Nui).**
  - The script "appears to have been an internal development". Some scholars think it was inspired by the Spanish annexation treaty
    of 1770.
  - "Literacy was a privilege of the ruling families and priests": a chiefdom, with a small literate elite
    ([Rongorongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongorongo)).
  - Ferrara et al. 2024, *Scientific Reports* 14 (doi 10.1038/s41598-024-53063-7;
    [phys.org summary](https://phys.org/news/2024-02-evidence-independent-written-language-easter.html),
    [Bologna record](https://cris.unibo.it/handle/11585/955616)) dated the wood of one tablet to the mid-15th century, before
    European contact. That dates the wood, not the carving.
  - Verdict: a possible independent invention by a chiefdom without towns, and the one real edge case.
- **Vinča symbols (c. 4500-4000 BC).** "It is unlikely that the Vinča symbols represent an early writing system"; it is "not likely
  that the stateless societies of Neolithic Europe would have had cause to independently invent writing"
  ([Vinča symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin%C4%8Da_symbols)).
- **Jiahu signs (c. 6000 BC).** "There is no consensus on the nature of the Jiahu signs"; critics call them "too primitive and
  inconsistent" to be writing ([Jiahu symbols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiahu_symbols)).
- **Cherokee syllabary.**
  - Sequoyah made it c. 1821, and "by 1825, the majority of Cherokees could read and write"
    ([Cherokee syllabary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee_syllabary)).
  - The Cherokee already had a National Council. It adopted a constitution in 1827
    ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/cherokeenation1820s.htm)).
  - Sequoyah knew of English writing: an invention under contact, inside a near-state.
- **Vai syllabary (Liberia, 1830s).** Momolu Duwalu Bukele of Jondu made it, under contact with literate settlers and missionaries
  ([Vai syllabary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vai_syllabary)). The lane did not source Vai political organisation.
- **Yugtun script (Alaska, c. 1900).** Uyaquq, a Yup'ik speaker, made it, starting from mnemonic pictographs for preaching the
  Bible. It was "influenced by" the Latin alphabet ([Yugtun script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugtun_script)). This is a
  stateless people, but the script was invented under strong missionary contact.
- **Bamum script (1896-1910).** King Njoya of the Kingdom of Bamum devised it: a state again
  ([Bamum script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamum_script)).

Michael Cook's point (Holberg, above) is that these "tribal literacies" lasted "without coercive institutions like compulsory
education systems" and had no institutional backing.

**Caveats.**
- The fetched rune and Ogham pages do not themselves say "no towns" or "chiefdoms".
- The Mangyan's political organisation was not sourced.
- Most sources here are encyclopedia summaries. The lane could not fetch Britannica's or the Met's pages (429 errors).

## 4. What it means for the model (arm S)

- **Arm S as built on main (lane 207, D60 B as built).** A state tech (writing, kingship, law, taxation, census, bureaucracy,
  coinage) is invented, taken up, shared or inherited only by a people with a town of its own (`techtree.Calendar.settled`).
  Without a footprint at all, a people that is not mobile counts as settled.
- **The record above:**
  - Inventing is town or state work, with rongorongo as the lone chiefdom edge case. D62 A keeps that rule.
  - Taking up is not town work. Chiefdoms without towns (runes, Ogham) and herders under chiefs (Tuareg) wrote, in small ways.
  - Groups with no chiefs at all are not in the record as writers.
- **Daniel's own wording, "towns or chiefdoms of its own" (D60 B), matches this, read as "is a chiefdom"** (a chief's seat ruling
  land), not as "knows the idea".
  - The model's chiefdoms *tech* is held by 96-100% of peoples by 500 years ago.
  - Letting it open the gate for taking up makes 89-90% of the peoples without towns write, foragers and herders included
    ([round4.md](round4.md), against main e6cb463c).
  - So that variant was not built. The choice between towns only and a chief seat of its own is in the inbox draft
    `draft-chiefdom-seat-uptake.md`.
