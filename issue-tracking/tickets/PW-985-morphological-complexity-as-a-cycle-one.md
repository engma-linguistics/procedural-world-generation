## Description
Owner 2026-10-28. No built-in decay ('complex past, simple present' is an artefact of which Indo-European branches are documented). Morphology erodes and periphrasis grammaticalizes into new morphology; direction follows sociolinguistic typology (Trudgill, McWhorter, Lupyan & Dale): large populations with many adult second-language learners shed opaque morphology (conquest substrates, city mixing, trade hubs), small tight groups accrue it. First languages start low (emerging sign languages build grammar over a few generations), so language age and complexity are separate (PW-947). A tendency to nudge with, not a rule. Inputs from demes (PW-981) and contacts; drives how much inflection PW-982's grammar layer shows.
- [ ] empire and trade-hub languages trend simpler, isolated small demes richer, across several seeds
