## Description
**In plain words.** You asked whether war, or plague, could keep the humans and kobolds down. The life tables say deaths alone cannot.
A people that breeds fast makes up its losses fast. If war killed an extra 0.5% of adults every year:
- humans would keep 94% of their numbers;
- kobolds would keep 98%;
- dwarves would keep 86%;
- elves would keep 46%.

An extra 1% a year of disease would leave humans at 77%, kobolds at 93%, dwarves at 42%, and the elves extinct. A plain "more war,
more plague" setting would wipe out the slow peoples first. Births depend mostly on women, so war's deaths of young men matter even less.

What does hold a fast breeder down is losing land, for a while or for good:
- **Plague** empties the densest, most town-bound peoples' towns for a century, and their neighbours move in and keep some of it.
- **Crowded, stateless war** leaves fields unworked.
- **Drought** cuts the food the land gives.

The design builds every counter-pressure that way, with a "how warlike" and a "how often plagues" dial per era. Even so, it would
trim the humans' share by a few to perhaps 25 percent, not halve it. As for kobolds, if they are too many it is because of their
density or their country, and no war rate changes that. Their density setting, or lane 78's share band, is the honest lever for them.

Questions:
1. Is "land, not deaths" the brake you want, with the dials to strengthen it per era? Or do you want deaths strong enough to bite on
   fast breeders, accepting that elves and dwarves then need protection (e.g. a lower war and plague exposure)?
2. Should "virgin soil" losses be on by default (realistic, and they help the dense farmers spread, the opposite of a brake), at 0.6 of
   the Americas' 90%?
3. What fall in the commonest people's share should the lane 77 suite expect at the default dials? That threshold is yours.
