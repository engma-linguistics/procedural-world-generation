## Description
In plain words: real languages build their question words from a few shared stems (English wh-: who, what, where,
when, why, which; the pointing words share th-: there, then, this). Ours draw each one separately, so the family
resemblance between them is missing. Owner ruling (desk card p.qscope, option B): A now (done in PW-1635: 'when' with
its own time ending, 'who' as 'what person'), then the full grid after the conquest and trade work (done in lane 13).

What to build (about two lane-days): each proto draws question and pointing stems crossed with person, thing, place,
time, manner and reason; the cells wear down and are renewed as a set; WHY, WHICH and THEN join the word list; the
target share of shared question stems comes from a sample of real grammars. The sketch page shows the table.
Indefinites (someone, anywhere, nobody; Haspelmath's map) are option C, not asked for now.

What misleads if never done: every language's question words look unrelated to each other, which no real language
does.
- [ ] stems x categories grid per proto, inherited and renewed as a set
- [ ] share of question words sharing a stem within the real range
- [ ] grid table on the sketch page
