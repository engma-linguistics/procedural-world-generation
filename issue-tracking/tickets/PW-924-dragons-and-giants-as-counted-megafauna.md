## Description
Owner 2026-10-27 (agreed with the coordinator's proposal). Dragons and giants just need to be where they belong. It is "less of a simulation", by design.

**1. Counted, not mapped as ranges.**
- Density from body mass by Damuth's law (density ~ M^-0.75), with apex hunters far sparser (Carbone & Gittleman 2002).
  - Dragons: ~1 per 1,000-10,000 km2. The exact value is set by the density trial ticket.
  - Giants: ~1 per 100-500 km2, in steadings of 5-20.
- Small populations stay viable through long generations: dragons ~200 yr, giants 50-80 yr. This uses the persistence-horizon founder rule plus PW-917 life history.
- Exempt from the 300-cell people target. Their target instead: present in most of the world's suitable country for their kind.

**2. Each kind sticks to its country, reaching many separate patches by flight or long range.**

| Kind | Country |
|---|---|
| black dragons | swamps |
| green dragons | forests |
| white dragons | cold mountains |
| blue dragons | deserts |
| red dragons and fire giants | volcanoes (they compete) |
| frost giants | tundra and ice |
| hill giants | hills |
| storm giants | coasts |

- Planned overlaps fit the ancient giant-dragon enmity:
  - frost giants with white dragons;
  - fire giants with red dragons;
  - storm giants with bronze or blue dragons on coasts;
  - cloud giants with silver dragons in high mountains.
- Otherwise the two mostly keep apart, because their country differs, with some chance of overlap at the edges.

**3. Groups, not lone animals.**
- Dragons: mostly solitary, an occasional mated pair with young.
- Giants: families and steadings.
- A later zoom places lairs and steadings.

Breeding stays a mystery, like eels before the Sargasso Sea: new dragons just turn up now and then. No mating sites are modelled.
