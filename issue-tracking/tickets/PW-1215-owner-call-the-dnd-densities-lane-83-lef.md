## Description
**In plain words:** lane 83 set each people's number per km² by its way of life (owner ruling D20 (4)). The forager numbers have a
source: Binford's 339 hunter-gatherer societies. The elf and dwarf numbers come from your ranges. The numbers below have no source.
I kept them as judgements, and each needs a yes or a number from you.

| people | per km² | way | why it was kept |
|---|---|---|---|
| halfling | 12 | farmers | small folk pack dense (PW-1149 B); the same as the human farmers |
| gnome | 12 | (none) | PW-1149 B: "gnomes stay at 12" |
| hobgoblin | 4 | agro-pastoral (new) | a settled warrior people with fields and herds, as in seven_meals; the tree listed them as hunters |
| dragonborn | 2 | (none) | the tree gives no way of life |
| human_seafarer | 0.7 | fisher-foragers | the top tenth of Binford (0.67): rich-coast fishers |
| drow 2, sea_elf 1.5, triton 1, goliath 0.15, giantkin 0.1 | | | not touched |

Options:
- A. Keep these numbers.
- B. Give each one a way of life and read its number from a source (e.g. hobgoblins as agro-pastoralists).
- C. Make small folk denser by body size (Damuth's rule, PW-1149 B's optional part).

Lean: A for now. Since stage 1 the contest doesn't read density, so these numbers set head counts, not who holds the land. Since stage 3
(PW-1203 A) every farming people, halflings and hobgoblins among them, lives at forager density (0.35) until it has crops, and at
gardener density (at most 5) until it has the plough. So these numbers apply only to farming peoples in the farming era.

Hobgoblins rose to 2.6–19% of all people in lane 83's screens (from 1.1–1.9%), with the martial contest and their new fields and herds.
If that is too many, lower their density or their contest, not the rule.
