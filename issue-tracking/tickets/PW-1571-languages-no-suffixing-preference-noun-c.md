## Description
In plain words: Daniel saw gender agreement at the front of a word. Both sides are supported (a gram's side comes from where its source word stood), and concord reaches adjectives, demonstratives and the verb (341 of 617 living lects have some concord; 211 all three). But the world has no suffixing preference.

Living lects, a24559: bound class/gender markers prefix 617 vs suffix 529 (sex-based gender: prefix 171 vs suffix 115); subject agreement prefix 298, suffix 256, both 30; object agreement suffix 236, prefix 107. Real languages favour suffixes about 2.5 : 1 overall (WALS 26A, Dryer 2013), and sex-based gender is mostly suffixal (Indo-European, Afroasiatic nouns); prefixal class systems are a Niger-Congo hallmark, not the norm.

Mechanism candidates (nudge, not force): preposed grams fuse more slowly than postposed ones (Himmelmann 2014, asymmetric reduction; Hawkins & Cutler 1988 processing account), so a pre-host gram stays a clitic or free word longer and more often decays before binding.
Options: (a) asymmetric fusion rate (pre-side binding x0.4-0.5); (b) leave. Lean: (a), and re-measure 26A bins.
