- 2026-10-28: Owner 2026-10-28, notes for the language pass. (1) Owner's Claude Doc 'Ethnonyms: A Taxonomy for Culture Generation' (https://claude.ai/artifact/GDbAyM2ZHRYuXKkeMes8vS; Claude Docs id 7b3b1078-417a-4f09-a04d-862b94bd6877): endonym patterns E1-E12, exonym patterns X1-X9, name-change processes P1-P8, scholarly/archaeological names, a per-culture JSON name stack (endonym, glossonym, exonyms per namer with pattern/gloss/relay path/era, name history). Its etymologies are not source-checked: use the patterns, never quote them as fact. (2) Morphology runs in cycles, not 'complex past decays': erosion, then periphrasis grammaticalizes into new morphology (Romance future, Uralic and Indo-Aryan case from postpositions, Chinese tonogenesis). Direction follows sociolinguistic typology (Trudgill, McWhorter, Lupyan & Dale): large populations with many adult L2 learners shed opaque morphology, small tight groups accrue it. Model: one complexity scalar per language nudged by population, L2 share (conquest substrate, city mixing, trade hubs) and isolation; a tendency, not a rule. (3) First languages were probably low-morphology (Nicaraguan and Al-Sayyid Bedouin sign, homesign), so language age and complexity are separate. (4) The comparative method reaches ~6-10 kyr: in-world scholars reconstruct families only that deep; older links are disputed (Nostratic-style), so the true tree and the scholars' tree differ, like PW-731's 'what they are vs what they are called'. (5) Food words travel with food (tea/chai, tomato from Nahuatl): loanwords for crops, animals and dishes along trade and crop spread are the first thing the language pass should build for the culinary book (PW-728).
