## Description
In plain words: fine goods (spices, gold, gems, ornaments, mithril, tin) fade over 1,200 pack-animal km. A sea mile costs about 1/28 of a
pack-animal mile (ORBIS), so by sea that is tens of thousands of km before the 1% cut-off. Since lane 222 every good keeps looking until
none is left, so fine goods now reach almost every taker on the coast or a navigable river. The long-haul layer of the overlay is
1,500-1,800 stretches an era on average, 2,800-3,200 at the present, 60-75% of them sea lanes. It is tree-shaped (27-34 pieces an era,
only 7-15 loops), so it reads as a branching network, but not as "a few trunks".

If nothing is done, the overlay's default view is dominated by fine-goods sea lanes, and the coming craft goods (PW-1522) will add more.

Options:
- A: leave it. Indian Ocean and Mediterranean luxury trade did reach most coasts.
- B: a per-trip handling cost (loading, harbour fees) on top of the per-km cost, so a sea haul is not almost free over distance (needs a
  source, e.g. ORBIS transshipment costs).
- C: draw only the long-haul stretches above a higher bar by default, the rest faint, as the local layer is.
