## Description
In plain words: after the first six (PW-1658), more templates for families to draw from. Each is a pattern (position, generics, how a founder or feature enters), never Earth words:
- Norse: -vík 'bay' (Reykjavík 'smoky bay'), -nes 'headland', -ey 'island', -by 'farm', -fjord.
- German: -burg, -dorf, -heim, -hausen, -stadt, -feld; a Slavic layer under German spelling (Leipzig < Lipsk 'lime-tree place').
- Romance/Gallo-Roman: estate names in -acum that split by region (-ac south, -y/-ay north: one name, two daughters); Villa-, Castel-.
- Chinese: systematic names relative to features and directions (Hebei/Henan 'north/south of the river', Shandong 'east of the mountains'; directional capitals Beijing/Nanjing, borrowed as Tokyo 'eastern capital').
- Persian/Indic: -abad 'settled place' (Hyderabad), -stan 'place of', -pur/-pura and -nagar 'city', -kot 'fort'.
- Turkic: -kent/-kand 'city' (Tashkent 'stone city'); colour words for directions (kara 'black' = north) as flavour, flagged disputed.
- Nahuatl: locatives -tlan/-co/-pan ('place of abundance of': Mazatlán 'place of deer').
- Semitic (Hebrew/Aramaic): Beth- 'house of' (Bethlehem 'house of bread'), Kfar- 'village', Tel 'mound' (a ruin-mound name for settlements on ruins: we have ruins).
- Greek: -polis, and phrases heard as names (Istanbul < 'to the city').
- Polynesian/Hawaiian: Wai- 'water', Mauna 'mountain' (Mauna Kea 'white mountain'); Māori narrative names (the 85-letter Taumata… hill).
- Algonquian/Native American descriptive hydronyms ('great river', 'long tidal river', 'at the great hill') as the model for substrate river names.
