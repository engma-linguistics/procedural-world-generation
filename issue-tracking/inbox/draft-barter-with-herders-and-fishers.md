## Description
In plain words: lane 146 gave forager peoples a share of farm food from farming neighbours (PW-1386), as in the Congo forests. It
counts only farmers (not herders) as trading partners, and tops up only the hunting-and-gathering rung, not fishing on rich waters.
That is what the sources read cover: all are forest hunters trading meat, honey and labour with farming villages (Ichikawa 1983,
Kitanishi 1995).

Measured (round1.md, 6 D&D runs against main): 37-48% of the forager peoples' people live within reach of 5+ farmers per km2, but
traded food is only 0.2-2.4% of their food, because the forager peoples left by 1500 AD are mostly coastal fishers (tortles, tritons,
sea elves, lizardfolk) or herders (leonin), and only their hunting-and-gathering part is topped up. So as it stands the mechanism is
nearly invisible in the D&D preset; B or C is what would make it show.

Options:
- A. Leave it (farmers only, hunting and gathering only) until a source on forager-herder or fisher-farmer exchange is read.
- B. Count herders too (the den peoples' raids already count farmers and herders), with the same cap.
- C. Also top up fishing cells (fish for grain).

Trade-offs: B and C widen the effect with no number behind them; C would also raise the rich-coast fishers that PW-1385 keeps
unfarmed, which may already be dense enough. **My lean: A**, and source forager-herder exchange (e.g. southern African foragers beside
herders) before doing B.
